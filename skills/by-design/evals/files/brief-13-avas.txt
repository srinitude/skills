Acoustic vehicle alerting sound for a zero emission bus fleet. The regulation requires the sound below twenty kilometres per hour and sets minimum sound pressure and frequency content so a blind pedestrian can detect the bus and judge whether it is approaching or reversing. Buses must be able to take a new sound after they enter service. Design workshops run with vulnerable road user groups, other state jurisdictions, passengers, operators and drivers. The open tension is between detectability at the kerb and what a hundred buses an hour do to the soundscape of a street people live on, and whether the fleet should carry one recognisable identity or a sound that disappears into the background.
