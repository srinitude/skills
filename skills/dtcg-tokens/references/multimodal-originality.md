# Multimodal originality

Read this file in Steps 04, 06, 15, and 21. It owns the source identity graph and the separate originality, identifiability, uniqueness, memorability, provenance, and one-of-a-kind claims in `assets/originality-analysis-contract.json`.

## Build the graph

Create located nodes for each applicable modality. Record observations from pixels, time ranges, sound, text, interaction, material, code, data, or sensor evidence. Connect nodes with spatial, temporal, semantic, causal, hierarchical, rhythmic, material, interaction, crossmodal, tension, absence, or silence relations.

## Falsify identity

For each proposed identity relation, run at least one relevant control: remove it, replace it with a category average, strip a surface trait, flatten hierarchy or rhythm, shuffle timing, transplant a near-neighbor layout, mask detail, or preserve style while changing structure. Keep the relation only when its removal causes recognition or structural loss and matched distractors do not explain it as well.

## Test identifiability

Use same-category, comparable-finish distractors and a `none of the above` option. Record top-1 and top-k retrieval, confusion, hit rate, false-alarm rate, within-source consistency, between-source separation, and counterfactual identity loss. Apply relevant viewport, state, crop, scale, grayscale, detail, time, and noise transformations.

## Test corpus uniqueness

Freeze the corpus, date, representations, and thresholds before comparison. Compare exact bytes, normalized token graphs, appearance, layout, relations, grayscale, edges, topology, semantics, time, audio, touch, and crossmodal signatures when applicable. Save nearest neighbors and explain metric disagreement.

## Bound claims

Difference does not prove recognition. Recognition does not prove originality. Memorability does not prove either. Style does not prove authorship. `bounded_one_of_a_kind` may pass only inside its declared corpus and tests. `globally_unique` is always false.
