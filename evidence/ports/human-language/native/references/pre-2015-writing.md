# Writing before 2015

Use with [writing rules and output checks](writing-rules.md) when choosing a voice, checking genre fit, or comparing human writing with AI habits. The cutoff is **before January 1, 2015**. This replaces the earlier before-2020 cutoff. Modern AI studies remain in [human writing and slop](human-writing.md); they are not part of this historical sample.

## How this sample was checked

Use the exact old edition, not a present-day rewrite with an old title. Each row names a dated document, fixed version, or scan. The pages read are stated so an excerpt review is not mistaken for reading a whole book.

An archive's current web wrapper may change while its scan keeps the original words. A versioned paper pins one version even when later versions exist. A dated official PDF identifies an edition, but its date alone does not prove an unbroken history of identical file bytes. Keep those kinds of proof distinct. Do not treat a current file hash as proof of what was online years ago.

These are direct craft observations, not experiments that prove what all readers prefer. The sample spans major kinds of writing; it cannot cover every domain, language, culture, or writer. Historic prose can be unclear, false, biased, or badly suited to today's reader. Learn from the useful choices without copying those faults.

## Creative work, personal voice, and reporting

| Fiction: [Jane Austen, Pride and Prejudice, volume II](https://archive.org/download/JaneAusten-PrideandPrejudice-1sted-1813-vol2/JaneAusten-PrideandPrejudice-1sted-1813-vol2.pdf) | T. | Personal letter: [Frederick Douglass to Harriet Tubman](https://archive.org/download/scenesinlifeofha1869brad/scenesinlifeofha1869brad.pdf) | Letter dated August 29, 1868, printed in Bradford's 1869 Scenes in the Life of Harriet Tubman, pages 6-8. | Specific reasons make praise meaningful. Parallel clauses and contrasts carry the argument. This printed tribute is not a model for empty praise or proof of every claim in the surrounding biography. |
| Speech: [Lincoln's Gettysburg Address](https://archive.org/download/gettysburgaddres4299linc/gettysburgaddres4299linc.pdf) | Historical pamphlet catalogued 1894; two printed versions on PDF pages 2-3, with their source versions named. | Repeated dedication language connects past events to the audience's duty. Repetition can serve speech. This is a reprint with two identified versions, not a recording or a single certified transcript of delivery. |
| Journalism: [Nellie Bly, Ten Days in a Mad-House](https://archive.org/download/3304680.med.yale.edu/ten%20days.pdf) | Norman L. Munro, 1887; introduction and chapter I, printed pages 5-7. | An assignment, concrete limits, dialogue, and an open question create movement. Let changing action guide pace. Do not copy the book's sensational or outdated language into care or health writing. |
| Criticism: [Virginia Woolf, The Common Reader](https://archive.org/download/commonreader0000wool/commonreader0000wool.pdf) | Harcourt, Brace, 1925; title/copyright pages and opening essay, printed pages 11-12. | A recurring image links abstract ideas; a clear subject helps longer thoughts cohere. Use an image only when it explains something. Social caricature and elaborate syntax are not defaults for plain prose. |

This literary selection is small, English-language, and weighted toward famous published authors. It tests blanket claims by finding counterexamples. It does not measure an average human style or represent informal speech across cultures.

## Apply the observations

- Set the purpose and audience before choosing a voice. No single tone fits this whole sample.
- Keep exact terms for shared concepts. Vary rhythm without renaming the same thing for novelty.
- Use concrete details because they explain, prove, locate, or evoke something the task needs. More detail is not always better.
- Keep the author's stance and doubt. Plain prose can express a firm view or an unresolved question.
- Let the content set the pace. Repeated forms can aid memory or make a distinction; unnecessary repeats waste the reader's time.
- Use a small worked example when teaching a new idea. Keep claims, examples, and evidence clearly distinct.
- Treat genre features as choices. Subtext may help fiction; an unstated condition can break a procedure.

These observations reinforce the linked writing rules for context, exact data, voice, rhythm, and review. They do not create a rule to sound old-fashioned, copy a famous author, or remove modern technical words.

## Tests for transfer

Keep a useful repeated term in a rule; separate conjecture from evidence in a report; adapt the same facts for a notice and a personal message; preserve a quoted literary line; retain a purposeful short urgent sequence; and reject a smooth but unsupported conclusion. Use [validation cases](validation.md) and record actual outputs. Historical resemblance and detector scores do not establish success.
